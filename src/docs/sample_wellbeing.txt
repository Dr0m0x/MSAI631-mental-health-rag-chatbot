Managing everyday stress.
Everyday stress is a normal reaction to demands and pressure. Simple habits can
help reduce it: taking short breaks during the day, going for a walk, doing slow
breathing for a few minutes, staying connected with friends and family, and
keeping a regular routine. Breaking large tasks into smaller steps can make them
feel more manageable. If stress feels constant or overwhelming, talking to a
trusted person or a qualified professional can help.

Healthy sleep habits.
Good sleep supports mental and physical health. Helpful habits include going to
bed and waking up at consistent times, keeping the bedroom dark and quiet,
avoiding screens for a while before bed, limiting caffeine late in the day, and
getting some daylight and physical activity earlier in the day. If sleep
problems continue for a long time, it is a good idea to speak with a health
professional.

What is cognitive behavioural therapy.
Cognitive behavioural therapy, often shortened to CBT, is a widely used talking
therapy. The main idea is that thoughts, feelings, and behaviours are connected,
and that unhelpful thinking patterns can affect how we feel and act. CBT helps
people notice these patterns and practise more balanced, helpful ways of
thinking and responding. It is usually structured, practical, and focused on
specific goals, and it is often used for stress, anxiety, and low mood.

Looking after your wellbeing.
General wellbeing is supported by regular physical activity, balanced meals,
staying socially connected, doing activities you enjoy, and asking for support
when you need it. Small, consistent actions tend to help more than occasional
large efforts. Support is always available, and reaching out is a sign of
strength, not weakness.
